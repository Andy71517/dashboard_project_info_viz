COVID recovery and household well-being

Step 0 briefing for stakeholder discussion

We processed 610,755 HPS respondent records and joined 459 state-cycle summaries to BLS with no unmatched keys. HPS covers January–September 2024; the BLS recovery timeline covers 2019–2024.

Employment figures and household experiences can move differently. The charts below describe the observed patterns. They do not establish that COVID, unemployment, or hardship caused a person's loneliness or anxiety.

The HPS sample size changes across 2024 cycles

The nine cycles contain 610,755 respondent records. Cycle 9 has 51,280, compared with 68,544 in Cycle 1. These are survey sample sizes, not changes in the number of adults in the population.

Source: Census HPS 2024 respondent CSVs; measured raw row counts.

State unemployment rose sharply in 2020, then declined

The selected states' 2020 peaks were California 16.1% (Apr); New York 16.7% (May); Texas 12.8% (Apr). The shaded area marks March–December 2020; the timing alone does not identify a causal effect.

Source: BLS LAUS; seasonally adjusted monthly statewide rates.

Hardship differs across work and household income groups

In Cycle 9 (2024-08-20 to 2024-09-16), the highest displayed hardship estimate is 55.8% for Worked in last 7 days / Under $50k. Income is the prior year's household income; did not work is broader than unemployment. Error bars show 90% intervals.

Source: Census HPS; U.S. adults with valid responses; PWEIGHT and 80 person replicates.

Adults facing expense difficulty report more loneliness and anxiety

In Cycle 9 (2024-08-20 to 2024-09-16), frequent loneliness is 21.0% in the harder-to-pay group versus 8.2% in the other group; frequent anxiety symptoms are 26.9% versus 9.7%. These are descriptive comparisons, with 90% intervals; they do not show that hardship caused either outcome.

Source: Census HPS; national weighted valid responses within each hardship group.

Does lower state unemployment go with less financial hardship?

Across 51 jurisdictions in Cycle 9 (2024-08-20 to 2024-09-16), the descriptive Spearman rank association is 0.10. Using the midpoint month instead gives 0.13. Dashed lines are state medians, not official thresholds. Each dot is a state or D.C.; the relationship is ecological and its sampling uncertainty is not shown here.

Sources: Census HPS state estimates joined to BLS LAUS using state FIPS and aligned collection periods.

Not every state returned to its 2019 unemployment rate

21 of 51 jurisdictions had a published 2024 annual unemployment rate at or below their 2019 rate. Changes range from -2.3 to +1.4 percentage points. A recovered rate does not mean every household recovered; each state has equal weight in this histogram.

Source: BLS LAUS; published unadjusted annual average unemployment rates, 2019 and 2024.

What to question before writing the story

  • Which groups are missing or suppressed, and could nonresponse change the comparison?
  • Does using a midpoint month change the state relationship?
  • Does the national age pattern hold in the selected state?
  • Could a low unemployment rate coexist with high hardship because of wages, prices, work hours, or household circumstances that these charts do not isolate?

Methods and limits

HPS uses person weights and 80 person replicate weights. Error bars show 90% intervals. Percentages exclude invalid answers per outcome. We suppress estimates below valid n=100 or Kish effective n=30. This project rule is not an official certification. Income is 2023 household income; work means any paid/profit work in the last seven days. Broad ages are approximate. The surveys do not follow the same people over time. BLS state estimates are modeled and revised. State relationships are ecological and descriptive.
