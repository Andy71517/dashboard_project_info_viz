# Round 2 closeout — recorded stakeholder replies

**Review date:** 2026-11-21. **Status:** Both original Round 2 stakeholder replies received and reviewed; the original persona transcripts remain unchanged under [Persona1/conversation.txt](Persona1/conversation.txt) and [Persona2/conversation.txt](Persona2/conversation.txt). This is an analyst synthesis, *not* an invented transcript or a claim that the stakeholders reached consensus.

## Round 2: Maya Rodriguez (policy director)

The updated evidence did **not** change Maya's top priorities: Q1 (labor-market recovery), Q3 (work/income hardship), Q4 (hardship and social outcomes). It changed the permissible **level of action**. She favors national and Virginia contextual planning and coordinated referrals; she rejects state allocations based on weak unemployment–hardship correlations, untested ranks, and suppressed Virginia subgroups. For historical recovery, she prioritizes **employment and labor-force count/percentage changes relative to 2019**; unemployment is the companion context. For near-term job-service conditions, she favors current seasonally adjusted unemployment. These should not be combined into one need score.

Maya identifies the Virginia Cycle 9 “worked in last seven days / 2023 household income under $50k” hardship cell as a blocking estimate, and younger-age cells as additional limitations. **Source correction:** her reply calls “98” the *effective* sample size, but the exported [focus_state_estimates.csv](../reports/round1_followup/focus_state_estimates.csv) records **hardship valid n = 98** and **Kish effective n ≈ 49.23**. The cell is suppressed because the project rule requires valid n ≥ 100; it is not a zero estimate. The correction does not change her policy judgment.

Maya supports sorting by point estimate **for triage only**, with sample sizes, 90% marginal intervals, suppression flags and point counts, but not numbered significance ranks, comparative “ties,” or allocation based on a league table. Her suggested “tiers” must remain heuristic rather than significance classes.

## Round 2: Jordan Lee (nonprofit service manager)

Jordan still prioritizes hardship among recent workers and work/income groups (Q3), and access to voluntary social-support/mental-health referrals (Q4). A statewide hardship percentage and estimated number of adults provide **broad contextual information**. They cannot identify clients, select staffing locations, prove eligibility, or quantify unmet need. Before staffing or location changes, Jordan requires **unduplicated service requests and unmet demand by ZIP or county**, services sought, served/not served and reasons, referral capacity, waiting times, and access barriers.

Jordan also emphasizes that suppressed low-income recent-worker groups, ages 18–34, ages 65+, and other small state-by-subgroup cells remain operationally important; “suppressed” means unreliable here, **not no need**. Work in the last seven days is not the same as BLS employment/unemployment status. The income measure is **2023 household income**, not current individual income.

## Shared ground

- The state unemployment rate must not be used as a household-hardship proxy.
- Hardship among recent workers and social outcomes by hardship group are useful descriptive planning contexts, not causal evidence.
- State percentages, population point counts, valid n, effective n, and 90% intervals require clear definitions and qualifications.
- Cycle 8 marks a documented HPS frame/method change, but the magnitude of its effect on observed missingness and estimates is unknown.
- Suppressed cells and missing operational/local data must remain visible as limitations.
